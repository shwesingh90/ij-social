Property Management Software Pricing in Australia, Explained + Pm · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read Property Management Software Pricing in Australia, Explained How property management software pricing works in Australia: per-property, per-block, per-user and quote-based models, hidden add-ons and how to compare. Property management software is priced in four main ways: per property, per block or unit, per user, or by quote. Each looks cheap in a different situation and expensive in another, so the sticker price tells you little until you work out what you would pay at your own portfolio size, with the add-ons you would actually use. This guide explains each model and shows how to compare them like for like. The four common pricing models Per property You pay a set amount for each property under management, usually monthly. Cost rises in a straight line as the portfolio grows, which makes it easy to forecast and easy to pass on to owners. It is the fairest model for small portfolios, because you only pay for what you manage. The things to check are whether there is a base fee on top, whether the first property or the first few are priced differently, and whether vacant properties still count. Per block, unit or lot Common in strata and multi-unit work, this model prices by the building or the number of lots. For a manager with mostly houses, it may be irrelevant. For a manager with several large blocks, it can be cheaper than per-property pricing, or dearer, depending on how "unit" is defined. Ask whether a block with twelve flats is one billable item or twelve. Per user You pay for each staff login. This suits an agency with a small team and a large rent roll, and it punishes the opposite: a growing team managing a modest number of properties. It also changes behaviour. If every login costs money, people share accounts, which weakens your audit trail. Check whether owners, tenants, tradespeople and your accountant need paid logins or get them free. Quote-based No public price. You describe your portfolio and the vendor quotes. This is normal for large agencies with custom integrations and multiple offices, but it is a poor fit for a landlord with three properties who wants to know what the next year costs. A quote also makes comparison slow, because you are comparing a document with a price list. A worked example of per-property pricing To show the arithmetic, here are InvestorJoint's published prices. They are used only as an illustration of how per-property pricing behaves, not as a comparison with anyone else. The first property is free, permanently, with no credit card. Investors pay $8 per property per month from the second property. Agencies pay $60 per month (GST inclusive) plus $4 per month for each property after the first. | Scenario | Calculation | Monthly cost | |---|---|---| | Investor, 1 property | First property free | $0 | | Investor, 4 properties | 3 paid properties x $8 | $24 | | Investor, 10 properties | 9 paid properties x $8 | $72 | | Agency, 1 property | First property free | $0 | | Agency, 21 properties | $60 + (20 x $4) | $140 | | Agency, 51 properties | $60 + (50 x $4) | $260 | Notice the shape. Per-property pricing with a base fee gets cheaper per property as the rent roll grows, which is the right direction for an agency. Hidden add-ons to ask about The headline number is rarely the whole bill. Before you sign, ask each vendor about: Payment processing fees. Who pays them, how much, and on which payment types (direct debit, card, BPAY)? Are they charged to the tenant, the owner or you? Onboarding and data migration. Is there a set-up fee? Is importing your existing leases, properties and transaction history included or charged per record? Extra users and roles. Are staff, owners, tenants and tradespeople all free, or are some paid? E-signing. Are lease signatures included, or priced per envelope? Trust accounting and reconciliation. Is it in the base plan or a higher tier? This matters most for agencies holding owner money. Support tiers. Is email support included, and is phone or priority support extra? Minimum fees. Is there a monthly floor, regardless of how few properties you manage? Contract terms. Month-to-month or annual, and what notice is needed to leave? Data export. Can you take your data with you, in what format, and at what cost? How to compare like for like 1. Fix your own numbers. Pick your property count today and in twelve months. Add the number of staff logins you need. 2. List the features you will genuinely use. Rent collection, owner statements, maintenance, inspections, trust accounting, tax-ready reporting. Ignore the rest. 3. Ask each vendor for the total monthly cost at those two property counts with those features, including fees on a typical month of rent. Get it in writing. 4. Add one-off costs. Set-up, migration and training, divided over twelve months. 5. Check the exit. A cheaper product with a long lock-in and no export is not cheaper. Do the comparison in a simple spreadsheet with one row per vendor and one column per cost line. Which model suits whom A landlord with one to five properties: per-property, ideally with a free or very cheap first property. A small agency with a growing rent roll: per-property with a base fee, since your cost tracks your income. A large agency with several offices: a quote, but insist on an itemised one. Strata or multi-unit specialists: per-block, after checking the definition of a unit. If you are still deciding what category you need, start with the property management software overview . To try a real setup before paying anything, the free first property exists for exactly that. FAQ How much does property management software cost in Australia? It ranges from free for a single property to several hundred dollars a month for an agency, depending on the model, the portfolio size and the add-ons. The only reliable figure is the total you calculate for your own property count and feature list. Is per-property pricing always cheaper? No. For a large block or a big team on few properties, per-block or per-user pricing can come out lower. Run your own numbers under each model. Why do vendors hide their prices? Quote-based pricing lets a vendor tailor a price to a large customer, and it also avoids public comparison. If you manage a small portfolio, a published price list is a good sign. Should I pay annually to save money? Only if you have tested the product on real data first and you are confident in it. Month-to-month plans keep your options open.
