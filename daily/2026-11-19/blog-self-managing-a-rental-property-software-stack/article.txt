Self-Managing a Rental Property: The Software You Need + Pm · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read Self-Managing a Rental Property: The Software You Need What a self-managing landlord needs in Australia: rent collection, records, tax-ready accounts, maintenance and compliance, and when software beats a spreadsheet. A self-managing landlord needs five things: a reliable way to collect rent, one place for records, accounts that are ready for tax time, a way to handle repairs, and a way to keep track of compliance dates. A spreadsheet can cover some of this for one property. Software earns its place when you add a second property or start losing time to chasing, re-keying and searching for receipts. What self-managing actually involves When you manage your own rental you take on the jobs an agent would otherwise do. Someone has to collect rent and notice when it is late. Someone has to keep every receipt, invoice and statement. Someone has to arrange repairs, check that the property meets its state's minimum standards, and put the numbers in front of your accountant once a year. Rules for rent receipts, bonds, entry notices and minimum standards differ by state and territory, so confirm what applies to you with your state's tenancy authority. This guide is about the tools, and the guide to self-managing a rental property in Australia covers the rules side in more depth. The five things you need 1. Rent collection Chasing rent by hand is the task landlords most want to stop. What you need is a regular debit from the tenant's account on a set cycle, a record against the right lease, and a visible flag when a payment is late. Manual bank-transfer matching works, but it depends on you looking at the bank feed every week. 2. Records in one place Leases, condition reports, quotes, invoices and photos should all attach to the property, not live in email threads and phone galleries. The test is simple: could you find the 2024 plumbing invoice in under a minute? Keep records for as long as the tax rules require. The ATO's general rule is five years from the date you lodge the return that uses the record, and records behind a capital gain are kept for five years after the sale or other CGT event, so a property's purchase records can need keeping for many years. Confirm your own situation with your accountant. 3. Tax-ready accounts At tax time your accountant needs income, expenses by category, loan interest and (where relevant) depreciation, split by owner if you hold property jointly or in a trust, company or SMSF. Doing this from a shoebox in July is the expensive way. Tracking expenses from your bank feed through the year, against the right property and owner, turns EOFY into a review rather than a rebuild. 4. Maintenance Repairs need a trail: who reported it, who quoted, what you approved, what was paid. A tenant request with photos, a job with a status, and a paid invoice that lands in your expenses once the job is marked paid removes three separate manual steps. 5. Compliance dates Smoke alarms, electrical and gas safety, pool barriers and similar items vary by state. You need a checklist per property and a reminder before a deadline, not after it. Check the specific requirements with your state authority. Spreadsheet or software? A spreadsheet is a sensible start. It costs nothing and you control every column. Compare the cost of an agent against the cost of your own time before deciding. FAQ Can I really manage a rental without an agent? Yes, in every state, provided you follow the tenancy rules that apply. Confirm obligations such as bond handling, entry notices and minimum standards with your state tenancy authority.
