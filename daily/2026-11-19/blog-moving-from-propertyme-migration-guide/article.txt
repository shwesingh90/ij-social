Switching From PropertyMe: A Rent Roll Migration Guide + Pm · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read Switching From PropertyMe: A Rent Roll Migration Guide How to move a rent roll between property management systems: export, opening trust balances, parallel run, cutover day and what to tell tenants and owners. To switch from PropertyMe, or from any property management system, treat it as a rent roll cutover: export your records, rebuild opening trust balances from a clean month-end reconciliation, run both systems side by side for a short period, then switch on a planned day and tell tenants and owners in advance. The software you are leaving matters less than the order in which you do these steps. Most migration problems come from skipping the reconciliation or cutting over mid-month. This guide is deliberately system-neutral. For platform-specific detail, see our pages on InvestorJoint as a PropertyMe alternative and PropertyMe pricing compared . For a shorter overview, there is also how to switch property management software without losing data . Before you start: decide the cutover date Pick a cutover date at the start of a month, ideally right after a month-end close. A clean boundary means opening balances are simple: they are the closing balances you just reconciled. Avoid public holidays, EOFY and the week owners are normally paid. Work backwards from the date, leaving at least one full rent cycle for the parallel run. Step 1: Export what you can Ask your current system for exports of the records you will need: | Record | Why you need it | | --- | --- | | Properties and owners | Addresses, ownership splits, owner bank details, management agreements | | Leases and tenants | Rent amount, cycle, start and end dates, bond amounts, contact details | | Trust ledger and owner balances | To build opening balances and keep an audit trail | | Outstanding arrears | So collection continues without a gap | | Maintenance and inspection history | Open work orders and upcoming inspections | | Documents | Leases, condition reports and management agreements | Check your agreement with the current provider for notice periods and data export rights before you cancel anything. Keep a full copy of every export, including reports you do not plan to import. You may need them for audit and tax queries later. What InvestorJoint does with an export Based on the switching to InvestorJoint page: you can send a CSV export of your properties and tenancies and the team helps with the import, or you can load a CSV yourself from the property import screen. Check the current turnaround with the team. The CSV import brings in property addresses, tenant name and email, weekly rent, lease start and end dates and the bond amount. Owner details, rent cycles other than weekly, paid-to dates, arrears and opening balances are not part of it and are set up separately. Columns the importer does not recognise are ignored (the preview shows them as not mapped) and rows with errors are skipped and listed, so keep your full export and check every field after the import. Step 2: Build opening trust balances from a clean reconciliation This is the step that decides whether the migration is trustworthy. Before cutover: 1. Complete the month-end reconciliation in your current system so the bank balance, ledger and individual owner and tenant balances agree. See the end-of-month reconciliation checklist . 2. Clear or explain unpresented items and unallocated deposits. 3. Record each owner's closing balance and each tenancy's rent paid-to date. 4. Set these up as opening balances in the new system (they are not part of a property and lease CSV import) and reconcile again on day one. If the opening balances do not tie to the bank, fix that before you go live. Trust account rules differ by state, so ask your state regulator whether any sign-off or notification applies when you change record-keeping systems. Our trust accounting requirements by state overview points to each regulator. Step 3: Connect the bank feed A bank feed can bring in bank transactions without a separate transaction export. Trust ledger history and owner balances still come from your old system's reports, so keep those exports. Confirm with your bank and your auditor that the connection matches how you want trust transactions evidenced. Step 4: Review the imported data Do not rely on the import until you have checked it. Pick a sample of properties and check every field: rent, cycle, paid-to date, bond, owner split, management fee. Check ten leases against their source documents. Check that every active tenancy appears once, and that ended tenancies are not live. Step 5: Run in parallel Keep the old system as the system of record and enter or mirror a full rent cycle in the new one. Compare receipts, owner statements and the reconciliation at the end. The site's migration guidance suggests overlapping for a billing cycle or two before relying on the new system exclusively. The extra cost is whatever your old provider still charges during the overlap; InvestorJoint's first property is free, with no credit card. Step 6: Cutover day On the planned day: Take a final export and a final reconciled closing position from the old system. Freeze changes in the old system, so nothing is entered in two places. Confirm opening balances in the new system match the final closing position. Switch tenant payment instructions and portal logins as planned. Run a small test receipt and disbursement before the first full run. Rent, term and bond do not change because the software changed, and tenants should not need to re-sign their lease. Do check whether any existing direct debit or payment authority needs to be re-established. Whether they do depends on the payment method and the providers involved, so ask each provider before go-live. Step 7: Tell tenants and owners Tell people before cutover, not after. Tenants need to know the date, what changes (a new portal login, possibly new payment instructions) and what does not (the rent amount, due dates and lease). The site copy recommends giving tenants a heads-up before go-live. Owners need to know the date, that statements will come from the new system, and when they will see the first statement. Offer to walk through the first one. If their bank details are being re-confirmed, say so clearly so the request is not mistaken for a scam. Check with your state regulator whether any notice is required when an agency changes payment details or systems. After cutover Reconcile again at the first month-end, keep the old exports archived, and cancel the old subscription only after a clean rent cycle. Plan for several weeks end to end: a few days to export and import, then at least one full rent cycle in parallel. They get a new portal login, and a tenant paying by direct debit may need to set up their payment authority again. Their lease and rent amount are unaffected.
