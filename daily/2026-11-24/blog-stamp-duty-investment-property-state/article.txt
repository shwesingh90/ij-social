Stamp Duty on Investment Property by State 2026 | InvestorJoint + States · 23 Nov 2026 · 9 min read Stamp Duty on Investment Property: State-by-State Rates (2026) Investment property stamp duty varies wildly by state—from $29,440 in NSW to $21,330 in Queensland on a $600,000 purchase. Here's what you'll pay in every jurisdiction in 2026. If you're buying an investment property in Australia, stamp duty is your largest upfront cost after the deposit—and the bill swings by tens of thousands of dollars depending on which state you buy in. A $600,000 dwelling costs $29,440 in stamp duty in New South Wales, $21,330 in Queensland, and $31,070 in Victoria. Because investors are excluded from first-home concessions and face higher rates in some jurisdictions when purchasing through trusts or companies, understanding the precise rate schedule in your target state is not optional—it's the difference between a viable deal and an overpaid mistake. Why Investment Property Stamp Duty Differs From Owner-Occupier Rates Every Australian state and territory levies transfer duty (commonly called stamp duty) when real property changes hands. The rate is progressive: the more expensive the property, the higher the marginal rate on each additional dollar. Investors miss out on concessions. First-home buyers in every state enjoy reduced or zero duty on purchases below certain thresholds—sometimes saving $20,000 or more. Investment properties never qualify, meaning you pay the full general rate from dollar one. Foreign buyers pay surcharges. If you are not an Australian citizen or permanent resident, every state now charges an additional foreign-buyer duty surcharge—typically 7–8 per cent of the purchase price—on top of the base rate. That can double or even triple your duty bill. Trusts and companies may face higher rates. Some states impose a "landholder duty" or higher general rate when the buyer is a trust or company. Victoria, for example, has historically applied slightly different scales when the beneficial owner is a trust, though convergence with the general rate has occurred in recent years. Always check whether your entity structure triggers a surcharge; your conveyancer or accountant will confirm this at settlement. New South Wales: The Highest Duty Bill in the Country New South Wales charges the steepest stamp duty of any state for investment properties. General investor rates (2026): Up to $16,000: $1.25 per $100 $16,001–$35,000: $200 + $1.50 per $100 over $16,000 $35,001–$93,000: $485 + $1.75 per $100 over $35,000 $93,001–$351,000: $1,500 + $3.50 per $100 over $93,000 $351,001–$1,168,000: $10,530 + $4.50 per $100 over $351,000 Over $1,168,000: $47,295 + $5.50 per $100 over $1,168,000 A $600,000 investment property in NSW attracts $29,440 in duty. At $800,000, the bill is $49,440 —nearly 6.2 per cent of the purchase price. Foreign buyer surcharge: An additional 8 per cent of the dutiable value. On a $600,000 property, that's $48,000 extra, lifting total duty to $77,440. Trust and company purchases: No separate rate for discretionary family trusts; the general scale applies. However, if you are acquiring shares in a landholder company (one that holds NSW land worth more than $2 million), landholder duty at similar rates applies to the share value. Revenue NSW administers transfer duty and publishes current rate tables at revenue.nsw.gov.au . Duty is payable within three months of the sale contract date. Victoria: Premium Duty and Absentee Surcharges Victoria's investment property duty sits just above NSW for mid-priced properties, thanks to a sharp jump in the top marginal rate. General investor rates (2026): Up to $25,000: 1.4% of dutiable value $25,001–$130,000: $350 + 2.4% of excess over $25,000 $130,001–$960,000: $2,870 + 6.0% of excess over $130,000 Over $960,000: $52,070 + 6.5% of excess over $960,000 A $600,000 Victorian investment property costs $31,070 in duty. At $1 million, you pay $54,670 . Absentee (foreign) buyer surcharge: An extra 8 per cent of the purchase price. On a $600,000 property, that's $48,000 added to the base duty, for a total of $79,070. Trust and corporate buyers: The general rate schedule applies. Victoria's landholder duty rules capture corporate groups that own land-rich entities; if the company's landholdings exceed $1 million in unencumbered value and 60 per cent of its assets are Victorian land, acquiring a controlling interest triggers duty on the proportionate land value. The State Revenue Office Victoria provides calculators and guidance at sro.vic.gov.au . Duty must be paid before you can lodge a transfer at Land Use Victoria. Queensland: The Most Investor-Friendly Major State Queensland levies the lowest duty among the eastern-seaboard capital markets. General investor rates (2026): Up to $5,000: $0 (nil) $5,001–$75,000: $1.50 per $100 over $5,000 $75,001–$540,000: $1,050 + $3.50 per $100 over $75,000 $540,001–$1,000,000: $17,325 + $4.50 per $100 over $540,000 Over $1,000,000: $38,025 + $5.75 per $100 over $1,000,000 A $600,000 investment in Queensland costs $21,330 in transfer duty—$8,110 less than NSW and $9,740 less than Victoria for the same purchase price. Foreign buyer additional duty: 8 per cent of the dutiable value. On $600,000, that's $48,000 on top of the base $21,330, totalling $69,330. Trusts and companies: The general rate applies unless you're acquiring shares in a "relevant trust scheme" or landholder with Queensland land exceeding $2 million, in which case you assess duty on the proportionate land value under Chapter 3 of the Duties Act 2001 (Qld). Queensland Revenue Office calculators are available at qro.qld.gov.au . Duty is due within 30 days of the settlement (or liability) date. Western Australia: A Competitive Mid-Market Rate Western Australia's rate schedule sits between Queensland and the eastern high-tax states. General investor rates (2026): Up to $120,000: $1.90 per $100 $120,001–$150,000: $2,280 + $2.85 per $100 over $120,000 $150,001–$360,000: $3,135 + $3.80 per $100 over $150,000 $360,001–$725,000: $11,115 + $4.75 per $100 over $360,000 Over $725,000: $28,453 + $5.15 per $100 over $725,000 A $600,000 WA investment attracts $22,515 in duty—only $1,185 more than Queensland. Foreign buyer surcharge: 7 per cent of the purchase price. On a $600,000 property, add $42,000, bringing total duty to $64,515. Trust and corporate purchases: General rates apply. If acquiring an interest in a "landholder" (a private company or unit trust scheme holding WA land worth $500,000 or more, representing at least 60 per cent of total assets), duty is charged on the unencumbered land value at similar rates. RevenueWA administers transfer duty; see wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-finance/revenuewa . South Australia: Modest Duty, Low Foreign Surcharge South Australia offers one of the lowest foreign-buyer surcharges and reasonable general rates. General investor rates (2026): Up to $12,000: $1.00 per $100 $12,001–$30,000: $120 + $2.00 per $100 over $12,000 $30,001–$50,000: $480 + $3.00 per $100 over $30,000 $50,001–$100,000: $1,080 + $3.50 per $100 over $50,000 $100,001–$200,000: $2,830 + $4.00 per $100 over $100,000 $200,001–$250,000: $6,830 + $4.25 per $100 over $200,000 $250,001–$300,000: $8,955 + $4.75 per $100 over $250,000 $300,001–$500,000: $11,330 + $5.00 per $100 over $300,000 Over $500,000: $21,330 + $5.50 per $100 over $500,000 A $600,000 South Australian investment property costs $26,830 in duty. Foreign buyer surcharge: Only 7 per cent of the purchase price, the same as WA but lower in absolute terms than the eastern states' 8 per cent. On a $600,000 property, add $42,000 for a total of $68,830. Trusts and companies: The general scale applies. When a private company or trust acquires a majority interest in a "land-rich" entity (SA land holdings exceeding $1 million and 60 per cent of asset value), landholder duty is levied on the proportionate land value. RevenueSA provides duty calculators at revenuesa.sa.gov.au . Duty must be paid within two months of the contract or conveyance date. Tasmania, ACT, and the Northern Territory: Smaller Markets, Distinct Rules Tasmania uses a progressive scale topping out at 4.5 per cent on amounts over $725,000. A $600,000 investment property costs approximately $21,730 in duty. Tasmania's 8 per cent foreign buyer surcharge adds $48,000 on a $600,000 purchase. The State Revenue Office Tasmania administers duty; see sro.tas.gov.au . Australian Capital Territory operates a unique hybrid model: buyers can choose between upfront duty (the traditional model) or an annual land-tax-like charge. The traditional investor duty on a $600,000 dwelling is around $21,490 , with a 4 per cent premium (not a surcharge) for foreign buyers—making it one of the lowest foreign-buyer imposts in Australia. Investors often choose upfront duty to preserve borrowing capacity. ACT Revenue Office: revenue.act.gov.au . Northern Territory has the flattest and simplest schedule: a $600,000 investment costs roughly $22,840 at current rates. There is no foreign buyer surcharge in the NT. This makes the Territory attractive for offshore investors, though the market size and liquidity are far smaller. Territory Revenue Office: revenue.nt.gov.au . How Purchasing Entity Affects Duty: Personal, Trust, Company, or SMSF Your ownership structure can alter both stamp duty and ongoing land tax. Personal name or joint tenancy: You pay the general investor rate. No additional trust or corporate duty, but you lose asset-protection benefits. Discretionary family trust: In most states the trustee (usually a corporate trustee Pty Ltd) acquires title, paying general duty. Victoria and NSW do not impose a surcharge for discretionary trusts acquiring single residential properties, but landholder rules apply if the trust structure is part of a larger corporate group. Company (Pty Ltd): Acquiring a property directly in a company name attracts general duty. However, if you later sell shares in that company rather than the land itself, buyers may trigger landholder duty—so exit strategy matters. SMSF (self-managed super fund): SMSFs buying property pay the same investor duty as any other entity; super funds do not enjoy concessions on stamp duty (though they benefit from concessional income and CGT rates). Ensure your SMSF deed permits direct property acquisition and that the purchase satisfies the sole-purpose test and in-house asset rules. What This Means for Your Portfolio Stamp duty is a sunk, non-deductible cost—you cannot claim it as a tax deduction, depreciate it, or recover it on resale. When you're comparing two similar properties across state lines, the duty differential is real money that either stays in your bank or disappears into state revenue. Example: An investor choosing between a $600,000 unit in Sydney and a $600,000 unit in Brisbane will pay $8,110 less duty in Queensland. Invested at 6 per cent over ten years, that $8,110 compounds to $14,500—enough to cover a future vendor's marketing campaign or bridge a shortfall in your next deposit. If you're buying through a family trust or company, verify the entity's duty treatment before you exchange contracts. If your structure later expands to multiple properties, landholder thresholds can creep up and trigger unexpected duty on share or unit transfers.
