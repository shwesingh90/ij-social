Commercial Lease Management Software: What It Should Track + Leasing · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read Commercial Lease Management Software: What It Should Track What commercial lease management software should track: rent reviews, outgoings, options, make-good, incentives, bank guarantees and critical dates. Commercial lease management software should track the money terms that change over a lease (rent reviews, outgoings recovery, incentives), the rights and obligations that sit on dates (options, make-good, bank guarantees), and the critical dates that trigger them. Residential tenancy software is built around short fixed terms and prescribed notices. Commercial leases are longer, negotiated and full of clauses that need a date and an owner attached. One thing up front: InvestorJoint does not yet have a dedicated commercial lease workflow. We say what it does today near the end of this post. First, a concept-level checklist you can use to assess any tool. Why commercial leases need different tracking A residential lease usually has a fixed term, a rent amount and a set of statutory rules. A commercial lease might run for years with options to renew, rent that moves by formula, tenant contributions to the building's running costs and conditions on handback. Missing a date is not an inconvenience. It can cost real money or cost you a right. For the residential side of this, see lease management software . What commercial lease management software should track | Area | What to capture | Why it matters | | --- | --- | --- | | Rent reviews | Review dates, method, notice requirements, reviewed rent history | Rent changes by lease formula, not just by notice | | Outgoings recovery | Which costs are recoverable, the tenant's share, estimates versus actuals | Recoveries are a major income and dispute area | | Options | Option dates, notice windows, option terms | A missed window can end the right | | Make-good | Handback obligations, condition evidence, estimated cost | Large end-of-lease costs and disputes | | Incentives | Rent-free periods, fit-out contributions, how they unwind | Affects effective rent and early-exit calculations | | Bank guarantees and security | Amount, issuer, expiry, return conditions | Expired security leaves you unprotected | | Critical dates | Commencement, expiry, review, option, insurance and guarantee dates | Everything above hangs off these | Rent reviews: fixed, CPI and market Most commercial leases move rent through one or more of three mechanisms. Fixed increases. The lease sets a percentage or dollar increase on specified dates. The software should calculate the new rent and remind you before the date. CPI. Rent moves with a price index. The software needs to record which index, which period and how the calculation is rounded, then keep the result. Market review. Rent is reset to a market figure, often through valuation or negotiation. This is a process, not a formula, so you need to track notice dates, who is appointed and what was agreed. Many leases combine these, for example fixed increases with a market review at an option date. Some also include clauses that limit downward movement. Retail leases are regulated by state and territory legislation, which can restrict some of these mechanisms, so check the rules for the state where the premises are before relying on any of them. Outgoings recovery Outgoings are the building costs that the lease lets the landlord recover from tenants, such as rates, insurance and cleaning. Good tracking means a list of what is recoverable under each lease, how each tenant's share is worked out, an estimate during the year and a reconciliation against actual costs afterwards. Disputes usually come down to evidence, so attach the invoices to the period. Retail leases may be subject to state legislation that controls what can be recovered and how it is disclosed. Check the retail lease rules in the relevant state before you rely on a generic process. Options An option gives the tenant the right to renew, usually by giving notice within a window. The software should store the option term, the notice window and the dates, and alert before the window opens and closes. For the landlord, an open option is a forecasting problem, so track whether it is likely to be exercised. Make-good and handback Make-good clauses define what condition the tenant must return the premises in. Capture the clause, store condition evidence from the start of the lease and attach any estimates. The cost can be large, so it should show on the lease record well before expiry. Incentives Rent-free periods, fit-out contributions and landlord works change what the tenant really pays. Track each incentive, the period it covers and whether any of it is repayable if the tenant leaves early. Without this, a face rent figure overstates the income. Bank guarantees and security deposits Record the amount, the issuer, the expiry date and the conditions for return or call. Guarantees expire. A reminder before expiry is simple and valuable. Critical dates and reminders All of the above needs a single critical-dates view that shows what is coming up across the portfolio, who is responsible and what has been done. Look for reminders you can configure, a record of what was sent and the ability to see dates by property and by tenant.
