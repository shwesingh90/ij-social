Tenant Portal Features That Cut Calls to Your Agency + Pm · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read Tenant Portal Features That Cut Calls to Your Agency What a tenant portal should let tenants do for themselves, and which routine calls each feature removes, from rent history to maintenance requests. A tenant portal earns its place by answering the questions tenants otherwise ring the office to ask: what have I paid, did my rent go through, can someone fix this, where is my lease. Each feature that lets a tenant look something up or log something themselves is a call, email or text your team does not have to handle. The aim is not to hide from tenants but to make the routine questions self-service so staff time goes to the situations that need a person. Why tenants call in the first place Listen to a week of inbound calls at an agency and most fall into a handful of buckets. "Did my rent go through?" "Can you send me a receipt or a rent history?" "I need to report something that is broken." "What does my lease say about this?" or "Can you resend my lease?" "Where is the repair I asked about?" None of these needs a property manager's judgement. They need access to information the agency already holds. That is the case for a portal: it is a view onto existing records, not a new workload. What a tenant portal should do See rent history and payments A tenant should be able to open a list of what they have paid, when, and what the current position is. This removes the "did it go through?" call and most requests for a rent history. It also reduces disputes, because both sides are looking at the same record. See payment confirmation Where payments run through the platform, a tenant should be able to see each payment and its reference themselves, rather than emailing to ask. Rules on rent receipts and record keeping vary by state, so confirm your obligations with your state regulator. Log maintenance requests with photos This is the biggest single saver in most agencies. A tenant who can describe an issue and attach photos from their phone gives the property manager what they need to triage straight away, with no back and forth asking what the problem looks like. The tenant can then follow the request's status instead of calling to ask. View the lease and documents A lease and other documents the agency makes available should be retrievable by the tenant at any time. This removes the "can you resend my lease?" request, which is usually asked at the worst moment, such as when a tenant is applying for something and needs the document that afternoon. Get notified instead of having to ask A portal that surfaces updates, such as a maintenance job moving forward, answers the "any news?" call before it is made. The calls each feature removes | Tenant need | Portal feature | Call it replaces | |---|---|---| | Check rent was received | Rent history and payments | "Did my rent go through?" | | Prove payment to someone else | Rent history | "Can you send me a rent history?" | | Report a problem | Maintenance request with photos | The phone call, text or email describing a fault | | Check on a repair | Request status | "Has anyone been booked yet?" | | Find the agreement | Lease and documents | "Can you resend my lease?" | The benefit is not only fewer interruptions. A request logged in a portal is a structured record: property, time, photos, description. A request taken by phone is whatever the person on the call managed to write down. What a portal does not replace A portal is not a substitute for contact when something is sensitive. Arrears conversations, disputes about bond or repairs, and notices that need a human tone still need a person. If your agency handles arrears, our guide to rent arrears management covers how to approach them. A portal's job is to clear the routine questions so there is more time for these.
