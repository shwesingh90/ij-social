How to Choose Property Management Software in Australia + Pm · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read How to Choose Property Management Software in Australia A practical checklist for choosing property management software in Australia, with a decision table for agencies and owner-operators. Choose property management software by starting with the work you actually do, then testing each product against a short checklist: how rent is collected, how money is accounted for, who else needs a login, what it costs as you grow and how easily you can leave. An agency holding money for other people needs different things from an owner managing their own rentals, and the decision table below shows where those needs split. Start with who you are "Property management software" covers very different products. Some are built for agencies with a rent roll, owner statements and trust money. Others are built for a landlord with one or two properties. Buying the wrong category is the most common mistake: an owner-operator pays for agency machinery they never use, or an agency adopts a landlord app that cannot produce an owner statement. Write down three numbers before looking at any product: how many properties you manage, how many different owners you manage them for, and how many people on your team need access. Those three answers narrow the field faster than any feature list. The checklist Hold every product you are considering against these questions. Take notes on the answers; do not trust a memory of a demo. 1. Rent collection and arrears How is rent collected: direct debit, card, bank transfer matching, or only manual recording? If a debit fails or is reversed, what happens to the ledger? Can you see overdue rent the moment you log in, with a reminder action beside each tenancy? Who pays the transaction fees, the tenant, the owner or you? 2. Where the money goes Is every receipt recorded in a proper double-entry ledger, or only in a list? Can the software split a receipt between owner, agency and any fees? If you hold other people's money, how does it handle trust accounting, bank reconciliation and month-end approval? Trust rules differ by state, so confirm your obligations with your state regulator and then ask each vendor to walk through a month-end on their screens. 3. Owner and tenant experience Do owners get a live view and a statement, or a monthly PDF someone builds by hand? Can tenants pay, log maintenance with photos and see their ledger without phoning the office? Do tradespeople get their own limited login, rather than a shared email thread? 4. Maintenance, inspections and compliance Does a maintenance job run from request to quote to approval to paid, and does a paid job reach the books without re-keying? Can inspections be run from a phone, with area-by-area notes and timestamped photos? Are compliance dates tracked per property, with reminders? 5. Records and tax Can you export all of your data to CSV at any time? Can you import transactions, leases and properties from your current system? For owner-operators: does it support the ownership structure you use (individual, trust, company, SMSF) and produce a year-end statement your accountant can use? 6. Pricing and exit Is pricing per property, per user, per block or quote-only?- Which features sit behind a higher plan or an add-on? Is it month-to-month, and what happens to your data if you cancel? Your state regulator can tell you whether licensing applies. Test with real data, not a demo A sales demo uses clean data. Your records are not clean. Before committing, load one real property with its lease, rent history and an open maintenance job, then run a week of normal work. Check the screens you will use daily: the arrears view, the receipt screen, the owner statement, the inspection form on your phone. If a product offers a permanent free first property, that is the cheapest way to do this. Red flags Pricing you cannot find without a sales call, when you only manage a handful of properties. No data export, or export only on request. Trust or owner accounting described vaguely ("handles your finances"). Everything works on a desktop, but inspections and approvals need a phone. Features listed as available that the vendor cannot show you live. What about switching later? Choose with an exit in mind. A product you can leave in an afternoon with a CSV export carries far less risk than one that holds your history hostage. FAQ How many properties do I need before software is worth it? Software earns its place when you start losing time or accuracy to a spreadsheet: chasing rent by hand, rebuilding statements, or digging for a receipt at tax time. For many landlords that is a single property, which is why a free first property is a reasonable starting point. Do I need trust accounting software? If you hold money on behalf of owners as a property manager, trust accounting rules apply and differ by state. Confirm your obligations with your state regulator, then choose software that can show you how trust money is kept separate. Landlords managing only their own properties do not hold trust money. Is cloud software safe for tenant and owner data? Ask where the data is held, who can see it and how access is controlled. Ask for specifics rather than a general assurance, and read the vendor's security page if one is published. Should I choose the cheapest option? Choose the cheapest option that covers your checklist. A cheap tool that leaves you rebuilding statements by hand costs more in time than a slightly dearer one that does not.
