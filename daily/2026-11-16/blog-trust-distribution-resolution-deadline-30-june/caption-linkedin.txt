Trustees of discretionary trusts need a valid, written distribution resolution in place by 30 June each year. Our new article explains why the deadline is fixed, the two penalties for missing it (trustee assessment at top rates, or income flowing to a default beneficiary), common mistakes that invalidate a resolution, and the section 100A risk when distributions don't reflect genuine benefit.

For Australian property investors holding rental properties in a family trust, a late or invalid resolution can't be fixed after the fact, so the planning needs to happen well before June.
