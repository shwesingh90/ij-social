Self-managed super funds can borrow to buy property, but only through a limited recourse borrowing arrangement: the property sits in a bare trust and the lender's claim is limited to that one asset. Our new guide walks through what lenders typically approve, how the bare trust works at settlement, and the common traps around improvements, replacing the property, refinancing and related-party leases.

For Australian investors holding or considering property in super, getting the structure and record-keeping right from settlement day matters, because missteps are visible to your auditor and the ATO.
