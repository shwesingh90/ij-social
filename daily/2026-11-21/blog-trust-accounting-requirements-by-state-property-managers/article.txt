Trust Accounting Requirements for Property Managers by State + Trust · 18 Nov 2026 · 12 min read Trust Accounting Requirements for Property Managers by State A plain-English overview of trust accounting requirements for property managers across NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA, TAS, ACT and NT, and where to confirm the detail. Trust accounting requirements for property managers are set state by state, and each state has its own regulator, its own legislation and its own details. The general shape is similar everywhere: money you hold for owners and tenants stays in a separate trust account, every receipt and payment is recorded, the account is reconciled regularly, and someone independent checks the books. The specific rules, including how often and in what form, differ, so confirm each one with your state regulator before you rely on it. This is a general overview, not legal advice. The figures below were checked against official state regulator and legislation pages in November 2026. Rules, forms and deadlines change, so confirm each one with your regulator before you rely on it. Where we could not confirm a rule from an official source, we say so and point you to the regulator rather than guess. The concepts that appear in every state Almost every Australian regime for residential property management is built from the same four ideas. 1. A separate trust account Rent, bonds and other money held on behalf of owners or tenants is client money. It must not be mixed with the agency's own operating money. The account must be a trust account at an approved bank or authorised deposit-taking institution, and the detail of how it is named and opened is set in each state. If you want a plain-English introduction, start with what trust accounting is in real estate . 2. Receipting and a record of every movement When money comes in, you issue a receipt and record it against the correct owner or tenancy. When money goes out, you record what it was for and who authorised it. A complete trail from receipt to disbursement is what makes the rest of the system work. Several states require receipts to be numbered consecutively, but the format, timing and exceptions (for example for electronic transfers) differ, so check your state's rules. 3. Regular reconciliation Reconciliation means proving that three things agree: the bank balance, the trust ledger total, and the sum of the individual owner and tenant balances. The required cadence and deadline are set by each state. Monthly is the common pattern, but not universal: the state sections below give the figures we could confirm. 4. Audit or independent review Trust records are checked by someone independent of the day-to-day bookkeeping. In the states we could confirm, an annual audit is required where trust money is held, with the period, the lodgement deadline and the eligible auditors set by the state. The trust account audit checklist covers how to prepare. State by state: what the official pages say Use this section as a starting point for finding the right authority and the right question to ask, not as a rulebook. Section numbers and penalties are left out on purpose. New South Wales Regulator: NSW Fair Trading. The legislation is the Property and Stock Agents Act 2002 and the Property and Stock Agents Regulation 2022. Audit: annual, for the year to 30 June, submitted within 3 months and no later than 30 September. The auditor must be a registered audit company, an authorised company auditor, or a public-practising member of a professional accounting body, and must not be an employee or partner of the audited person in the last 2 years. Responsible person: the licensee in charge. Only a licensee in charge may authorise trust withdrawals, and this cannot be delegated. Receipting and reconciliation: we could not confirm the exact receipt, record-keeping and reconciliation rules from an official page, so check them with NSW Fair Trading. Victoria Regulator: Consumer Affairs Victoria. The legislation is the Estate Agents Act 1980 and the Estate Agents (General, Accounts and Audit) Regulations 2018. Account: you may open one trust account or several. Receipts: printed receipts in numerical sequence, marked "trust account", with a receipt register. Money is banked by the end of the next business day and the records are updated by then. Duplicates are kept for 7 years. Reconciliation: monthly, with the statement verified as true and accurate within 14 days of month end. Audit: annual, for 1 July to 30 June, completed within 3 months and lodged through myCAV within 10 business days of receipt from the auditor. The auditor must be a practising public accountant who belongs to CPA Australia, the Institute of Public Accountants or Chartered Accountants, and not the agent, an employee, a partner, or someone who held either role in the last 2 years. Responsible person: the principal agent, or the officer in effective control. Queensland Regulator: the Queensland Office of Fair Trading. The trust rules sit in the Agents Financial Administration Act 2014 (the Property Occupations Act 2014 deals with licensing). Account and receipts: money held for others must go through a trust account. Receipts are uniquely and consecutively numbered, and money is banked by the end of the first business day, with the receipt completed then. Records are kept for 5 years. Reconciliation: within 5 days after month end, comparing the trust account book, the trust ledgers and the bank statement. Audit: annual, with the report lodged within 4 months after the audit period ends (2 months in the last year of business). The auditor must be a registered auditor under the Corporations Act, or a member of CPA Australia, Chartered Accountants ANZ or the Institute of Public Accountants. Responsible person: the trust account holder (principal licensee). Western Australia Regulator: Consumer Protection, within the Department of Local Government, Industry Regulation and Safety. The legislation is the Real Estate and Business Agents Act 1978. The department was previously named differently, so older documents may show the old name. Account: every licensee keeps one or more trust accounts used only for the purposes of the Act. That can be one account for everything or separate accounts for sales, rentals and so on. Receipts: receipts carry a number in consecutive order, and cancelled numbers are accounted for. Electronic transfers do not need a receipt, but a record must be kept. Reconciliation: monthly, as at the last day of the month. The department's trust account handbook says to complete it within 10 working days after month end, signed and dated by the licensee or the person in bona fide control, even when the balance is nil. Audit: annual, for the calendar year, with the auditor delivering the report within 3 months (by 31 March). The auditor is approved by the Commissioner for Consumer Protection. Responsible person: the licensee, or for a company the person in bona fide control. South Australia Regulator: Consumer and Business Services. The legislation is the Land Agents Act 1994 and the Land Agents Regulations 2025. Account and receipts: trust money goes into an account at an authorised deposit-taking institution in the agent's name, approved by the Commissioner. Receipts are issued immediately on consecutively pre-numbered forms marked "Trust Account", or by an approved computer program. Records are kept for 5 years. Reconciliation: monthly, at the end of each month, comparing the cash book and the ledgers with the trust account. Audit: annual, by a registered company auditor under the Corporations Act or a person prescribed by regulation. An employee or partner of the agent in the last 2 years cannot audit. Responsible person: the registered agent. Tasmania Regulator: the Property Agents Board, supported by Consumer, Building and Occupational Services (CBOS). The legislation is the Property Agents and Land Transactions Act 2016 and its Regulations. Account and receipts: the trust account is kept in Tasmania and has "trust account" in its title. Receipts are consecutively numbered, and a receipt is not required for an electronic transfer unless it is requested. Reconciliation: at least once in each calendar month, comparing the cash book, the bank and the ledger. Audit: annual, for the 12 months to 30 June, with the audit completed within 3 months and the report given to the Board within 7 days of receipt. The Board may also order an audit at any time. Responsible person: the property agent (for a corporation, the property agent responsible for managing it). Australian Capital Territory Regulator: Access Canberra (the Commissioner for Fair Trading). The legislation is the Agents Act 2003. Account and receipts: a licensed agent keeps a trust account at an authorised deposit-taking institution in the ACT with "trust account" in the name, and may keep more than one. Trust money is paid in by the next business day of the institution. A receipt describing what the money is for must be given, except for direct credits. Reconciliation: we found a quarterly statement due within 15 business days of quarter end, given to the auditor. We could not confirm a monthly reconciliation duty, so check Access Canberra. Audit: required where trust money was held, for the financial year unless the Commissioner sets another period, within 3 months after the period. The auditor must be a qualified auditor under the Minister's determination. Responsible person: the licensed agent. Northern Territory Regulator: the Agents Licensing Board, under the Agents Licensing Act 1979 and its Regulations. Account and receipts: the account is titled with the agent's name plus "Trust Account", opened within 7 days of the licence being granted, and an agent may hold two or more trust accounts. Receipts are marked "Trust Account" and written from a numbered book with duplicates carrying the same number. Accounting records are kept for 6 years. Reconciliation: we could not find a reconciliation provision in the Act and sources disagree, so check the Board. Audit: annual, for the 12 months to 30 June (or another date lodged with the Registrar), with the audit within 3 months after the period. The auditor must be a registered company auditor, independent of the agent. Responsible person: the licensed agent. A report that looks right but is not the one your regulator asks for does not help at audit time. Frequently asked questions Do all states require a separate trust account? The general principle that client money is kept apart from agency money is common across Australia, and the states we checked require a dedicated trust account at an approved institution. Whether you may or must keep separate accounts for rent, sales or other purposes differs by state (Victoria, Western Australia and the ACT allow more than one, and the Northern Territory allows two or more), so confirm the detail with your regulator. How often must I reconcile my trust account? It depends on the state. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania set a monthly reconciliation, with different deadlines (Victoria 14 days after month end, Queensland 5 days, Western Australia 10 working days under the department's handbook). The ACT requires a quarterly statement within 15 business days of quarter end. For New South Wales and the Northern Territory, check the regulator. Do I need a trust audit every year? In the states we checked, yes, an annual audit is required where trust money is held, with the period, the lodgement deadline and the eligible auditors set by each state. Our audit checklist helps you prepare regardless of the state. Can software keep me compliant on its own? No. Software can record receipts, produce reconciliations and keep an audit trail, but the licensee remains responsible for compliance.
